Keka and the topic

Context you need in your head. Almost none of this belongs in the outreach itself.

Who Keka is

Keka is an HR and payroll software company. From their own site: they describe themselves as a people enabler covering payroll, core HR, performance, hiring and onboarding, time and attendance, and project timesheets, and they state they are trusted by 10,000+ organizations and used by 2.5 million employees. They serve technology and services, pharma and manufacturing, banking and financial services, and retail.

They are underwriting this series to build relationships with senior People leaders in the US market. Their interest is the long game, not leads from the room.

How to talk about the sponsor. Lead with the dinner and the conversation, never with Keka. If asked directly, the honest answer is: "Keka sponsors the series, they will be at the dinner as participants, and there is no pitch, demo, or presentation." Do not position this as a Keka event, do not send Keka product material, and do not promise anyone a Keka conversation. Attendees are asked once, after the event, whether they would like to talk further. That is the entire commercial motion.

The topic: Performance Theater

We're Measuring More and Developing Less. This is the thing that makes the invite land, so it is worth knowing well.

We have never measured performance more precisely, and we have rarely felt less sure it is working. Gartner finds 95% of HR leaders are unhappy with their traditional performance process, and 59% of employees say reviews have no impact on how they actually perform. Gallup finds only 14% of employees strongly agree their reviews inspire them to improve. More reviews, more feedback loops, more dashboards, and the honest question in most HR leaders' minds is the same: are our people actually getting better?

The three tables

1. The Measurement Trap

We track more about performance than ever. Is any of it actually making people better? Roughly $1,500 per employee per year goes into performance processes and software.

2. The Manager Gap

Development lives or dies at the manager level. Managers drive about 70% of the variance in engagement, yet only 39% of employees say their manager gives effective developmental feedback.

3. Rated vs. Grown

70% of HR leaders believe their performance system fails to identify future leaders. One in three employees already feel they could have more impact in a different role.

Every statistic above is sourced from Gartner, Gallup, or Deloitte and is already published on our event pages. You are safe to use any of them verbatim. Please do not invent or round new ones.

FAQ

The questions you will get. Answers you can use nearly word for word.

What actually is the Leadership EXchange?

An invite-only dinner for senior HR and People leaders, built around one topic and real conversation. It exists because there is almost nowhere for practitioners at this level to compare notes honestly without a vendor in the middle of it. The value is the room: people who are running the same problems inside organizations of a similar size and shape.

What is the format?

Reception with drinks from 5:30. Everyone sits around 6:30 at tables of six to eight. Tables run on guided prompts rather than a speaker, and Achieve seats the room deliberately so you are with people working on the same things you are. Done by 8:30.

It is topic-driven and structured, but there is real room to put your own question on the table. Some of the best conversations start when someone says "here is what I am actually stuck on."

Who else is in the room?

Internal practitioners only, Director level and above. CHROs, CPOs, VPs of HR and People, and the Directors who run this work day to day. Around 30 people, from organizations of roughly 250 to 5,000 employees.

Are vendors or salespeople allowed?

No. No vendors, no consultants, no fractional practitioners, and no sales or marketing leaders. This is the rule that makes the rest of it work, and it is enforced at the approval stage.

There is a sponsor. What does that mean for me?

Keka, an HR software company, underwrites the series. They will be in the room as participants in the conversation. There are no pitches, no demos, and no presentations at any point in the evening.

After the event you get asked once whether you would like to set up time to learn more about them. That is entirely your call, and saying no changes nothing about your standing in the community.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Dinner and drinks are fully covered.

How do I get there and park?

Covered. Attendees receive an Uber voucher, and valet parking is fully covered at venues that have it. Achieve sends the details the day before the event.

Do I get recertification credits?

Yes. The session is approved for SHRM and HRCI recertification credits, which are emailed to attendees afterward.

I registered. Am I confirmed?

Not yet. Registration is a request. Achieve reviews every one personally to protect the makeup of the room, then sends a confirmation directly. Most people hear back quickly.

Can I bring someone from my team?

Ask. If they are Director level or above and internal, it is usually a yes, subject to seats. Send the name and title over rather than bringing someone unannounced.

What is the topic this time?

Performance Theater: We're Measuring More and Developing Less. Three tables: the measurement trap, the manager gap, and the space between rating someone and actually growing them. Full detail is in section 04.

Will it be recorded, and is it on the record?

Nothing is recorded. The room is small specifically so people can be candid. Treat it as off the record unless someone says otherwise.
